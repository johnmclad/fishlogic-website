Fishing Grand Lake St. Marys through the seasons

Grand Lake St. Marys is a mid-depth reservoir with murky water, and the creek channels are the organizing feature: fish move up and down them with the seasons and position on the cover that sits closest to the channel. Largemouth bass are the primary target.

Pre-spawn fish follow the channels toward the backs of creeks and stage on secondary points, channel swings and the first brush outside spawning flats; crankbaits, lipless baits and jigs in 6 to 12 feet are high-probability choices as water warms through the 50s. The spawn happens on shallow flats and in pockets, and post-spawn fish drift back to the channel-side brush and points.

In summer a thermocline usually forms somewhere in the 15 to 25 foot band, and the productive brush piles, humps and ledges are the ones that sit just above it; Texas rigs, football jigs and deep crankbaits fished slowly along the channel edge are the staple. Stained or murky water favors bolder colors and baits that displace water. Fall pulls gizzard shad and bluegill and the bass that follow them back into the creeks, and a squarebill or spinnerbait on channel-side cover is likely to find them.

Winter concentrates fish on riprap, channel bends and the deepest brush near the channel, where a slow jig or jerkbait is the likely producer; this is not float-and-fly water, and slower is usually better than finer. Crappie stack on the deeper brush and stake beds through summer and winter and move to shallow cover to spawn in spring.

Seasonal notes describe how fish on this type of lake typically behave. They are a starting point, not a prediction for a specific day; the app supplies that each morning.

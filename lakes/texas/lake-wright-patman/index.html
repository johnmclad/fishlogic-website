Fishing Lake Wright Patman through the seasons

Lake Wright Patman is a southern lowland reservoir: stained water, shallow wood and grass, river-channel ledges, and current when water is moving through the system. Largemouth bass are the main target.

Pre-spawn here starts early and shallow. As water moves into the mid-50s, bass push toward grass lines, shallow wood and the mouths of spawning bays, where a lipless crankbait ripped through grass or a slow-rolled spinnerbait is a high-probability pattern based on seasonal behavior. The spawn happens in shallow bays around wood, stumps and grass, and post-spawn fish gather on the first channel swings and brush piles outside those bays.

Summer is ledge season: fish set up on river-channel ledges, humps and brush in 12 to 25 feet, and the bite is often tied to current, so moving water tends to be the signal to be on a ledge with a deep crankbait, big worm or football jig. The stained water here favors chartreuse, firetiger and dark high-contrast baits. Fall scatters bass shallow again as shad, bluegill and crawfish move into creeks and pockets, and shallow wood and grass edges become likely holding areas.

Winters are mild for a reservoir, and fish often stay on channel swings, riprap and the deeper edges of the cover they used in fall rather than moving to true deep water; a slow jig, jerkbait or crankbait along those edges is the likely play. Crappie use the brush piles and stake beds along the channels year-round and move shallow around wood and grass to spawn in early spring. Striped bass work the main river channel and tailrace areas with the shad, going deep in summer heat and feeding most actively in cooler water.

Seasonal notes describe how fish on this type of lake typically behave. They are a starting point, not a prediction for a specific day; the app supplies that each morning.

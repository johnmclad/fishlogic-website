Fishing Lake Hatchineha through the seasons

Lake Hatchineha is a shallow vegetation lake: tea-colored, tannin-stained water, broad flats, and the fishing is organized by grass rather than depth. Hydrilla, lily pads, reeds and floating mats are the structure here. Largemouth bass are the target, with crappie a strong second, and shad, shiners and bluegill are the primary forage.

In a warm climate the spawn comes early and stretches out; bass move to the inside grass lines, pad fields and hard-bottom pockets in the vegetation as water settles into the 60s, and a wacky-rigged stick bait, swim jig or a slowly worked swimbait along those edges is a high-probability pattern based on seasonal behavior. Pre-spawn fish stage on the outside grass edges and in the open-water holes inside the hydrilla.

Through summer and into fall the mats take over: a hollow-body frog across the surface and a heavy punch rig through the thick stuff are the two presentations that reach fish under the canopy, and the outside edge of the hydrilla in the deepest water the lake offers is where fish are likely holding on bright days. In this dark water, black-and-blue and junebug colors and a bulky profile help fish find the bait in the grass. Fishing a vegetation lake never sends you deep; it sends you to a different kind of grass.

Winter is mild and the fish stay in the grass, favoring the deeper outside edges and any open-water holes on cold mornings and moving back inside as the day warms. Crappie school in the open water and along the outside grass edges in cooler months and move into the pads and reeds to spawn.

Seasonal notes describe how fish on this type of lake typically behave. They are a starting point, not a prediction for a specific day; the app supplies that each morning.
